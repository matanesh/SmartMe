# Product assumptions

## The job

Someone opens the app during a small pause and leaves with an interesting idea they can recall, share, or use. A satisfying visit should take two to five minutes. The promise is immediate discovery in native Hebrew; books are one source among research, facts, history, and practical editorial ideas.

**Working name: רגע.** A short Hebrew name fits the moment of use. SmartMe remains the repository name. Neither name nor brand has been market-tested.

## Current experience

The feed opens immediately, without an onboarding questionnaire. The first batch deliberately alternates psychology, habits, science, money, work, technology, relationships, and history. Ten content formats and a small set of editorial treatments provide variation without asking users to learn a new interaction for every card.

`בשבילך` currently means the mixed editorial selection. It is the same selection for everyone; likes do not secretly rank or personalize it. Topic chips act as immediate filters. There is no separate interests onboarding screen yet.

Five-minute sessions contain five related cards. The time label includes reflection and interaction, and is a product target rather than a timer or measured completion claim. People can leave, resume, revisit the previous card, or replay a finished session.

Saved is a simple personal collection on this device. Interesting is a private signal with no invented popularity count. Sharing opens the native share sheet where supported, otherwise copies text and a direct idea link. Localhost links are useful only where that host is reachable.

Audio tests the interface and whether the format interests people. All three episode demos are silent and visibly labelled. Episode titles are proposals for future recordings, not claims that finished audio exists.

## Learning without pressure

The daily count starts at zero. A unique idea counts when someone explicitly taps `לקחתי משהו`, reveals an answer, answers a quiz, or advances a session. These are interaction acknowledgments, not proof of comprehension. Revisiting that idea on the same day does not add another count. The device's local date defines “today.”

There is no streak, leaderboard, reward currency, or notification pressure. The feed loads in batches and eventually offers a natural stopping point. The experiment should measure whether people feel the time was worthwhile, not merely whether they stay longer.

## Editorial assumptions

Forty realistic Hebrew cards are sufficient to judge the early feel, but insufficient to test long-term retention. Each topic currently has five cards. Most reading estimates are 20–30 seconds; these are editorial estimates that need native-reader timing.

Source-based cards use original paraphrases with links. Practical suggestions and illustrative stories are attributed to the prototype's editorial voice. Original quotes are explicitly credited to that voice, not to famous people. Research claims are kept qualified; isolated findings are not universal rules.

External factual sources were checked selectively during authoring, including NASA, CERN, Smithsonian, James Clear, and original research references. This is not a completed publication-level fact check. Any pilot with new content needs a Hebrew editorial and source review.

## Explicitly outside this version

Backend, authentication, Supabase setup, cloud sync, recommendation models, analytics collection, push notifications, social profiles, subscriptions, an editorial CMS, content uploads, and audio generation. None is necessary to test whether a short Hebrew knowledge visit feels compelling.

## Questions the prototype should answer

1. Can a new reader find a worthwhile idea within the first minute?
2. Does format variation feel enjoyable and coherent, or fragmented?
3. Is a five-card session more satisfying than open-ended discovery?
4. Do people return to saved ideas, or only collect them?
5. Which Hebrew hooks sound natural and which feel translated or overstated?
6. Will audio with actual Hebrew recordings outperform the same ideas in text?
